Why is the Encore ESP the simple starting choice?

The Baratza Encore ESP gives a new Bambino owner a clearly defined espresso region on its adjustment dial. Its included dosing cup supports the small-portafilter workflow, avoiding the need to balance the Bambino portafilter under an unsupported chute while learning to measure a dose.

Use the cup as a transfer tool, not a substitute for weighing. Weigh the grounds that reach the basket, especially after a grind change or a switch between coffees. A little coffee can remain within a grinder or emerge from a previous dose; we have not measured retention for this comparison and do not promise zero waste. What matters is knowing whether the basket received the intended amount.

The stepped adjustment is easy to record: your coffee notebook can say which position you used. Its limitation appears when a change feels larger than you want for a particular bean. Before deciding the grinder is inadequate, keep the dose and puck preparation consistent and compare the result. Do not change grind, dose, and shot stopping point simultaneously, because you will lose the information needed to decide what helped.

  • Pros: a defined espresso adjustment region; cup-based transfer; uncomplicated controls for learning.
  • Cons: fixed steps rather than continuous adjustment; manual dose control; switching brew methods adds checking and cleanup.

Skip this if you specifically want a programmable timer or need continuous adjustment between marked positions. Also check the product name: Encore, Encore ESP, and Encore ESP Pro are different grinders.

Who should choose the Smart Grinder Pro instead?

The Breville Smart Grinder Pro fits a repeatable hopper-and-timer routine. Its small cradle supports 50–54mm portafilters, and the controls let you save a grinding duration. That is useful when one household mostly drinks one coffee and wants fewer loose pieces on the counter.

The timer measures seconds, not grams. A stored duration may produce a different amount after you change roast, grind size, or other conditions. Establish the dose with a scale first, then use the timer to get close and check it periodically. Someone expecting automatic grind-by-weight operation should buy a grinder that explicitly offers that function; a digital screen and programmable dose do not establish it.

Breville also documents an adjustable upper burr. Treat that as part of the manufacturer's setup process if the ordinary range does not meet your needs, rather than an invitation to change internal and external settings every morning. Follow the manual when cleaning or adjusting, note the original position, and alter one variable at a time. This keeps the grinder understandable when another person in the household uses it.

  • Pros: timed operation; a compatible small-portafilter cradle; a clear path for repeated doses of the same coffee.
  • Cons: timer settings need recalibration; the hopper workflow is less tidy for frequent bean changes; extra controls can encourage changing too many variables.

Skip this if your main goal is weighing a different single dose of beans for every drink with minimal workflow overhead, or you expect the timer to deliver an exact mass without checking.

Does the basket change what you need from the grinder?

Yes. Breville distinguishes single-wall baskets for freshly ground coffee from dual-wall baskets that add pressure control. The basket in your portafilter changes how informative shot flow is when adjusting the grind. Check what your machine actually included, because regional packages and replacement baskets can differ.

With a single-wall basket, the coffee bed has a central role in restricting flow. An espresso-capable grinder therefore needs adjustments that let you move between a shot that runs too quickly and one that barely flows. A label saying a grinder has an “espresso” setting does not demonstrate that its adjustment steps suit this task. Neither a large advertised setting count nor a small number on a dial is meaningful across different grinder models.

With a dual-wall basket, avoid treating crema or a preset shot button as proof that the grind is optimal. The basket can make different coffees behave more similarly. If you intend to move to single-wall preparation, choose the grinder for that future workflow now. If you are happy with occasional preground coffee and a dual-wall basket, buying a new grinder is optional rather than a required machine upgrade.

How should you dial in without wasting the first bag?

Start with a dose appropriate to your particular basket, keep it fixed, and weigh the espresso you produce. Use the grinder maker's espresso starting guidance, then adjust from the result. Do not copy someone else's exact number: calibration, beans, basket geometry, and coffee age all change the useful setting.

As an illustrative recipe, 18 grams of dry coffee and 36 grams of beverage is a 1:2 ratio. It is not a requirement to force 18 grams into every Bambino basket. Follow the basket guidance, leave suitable headspace, and choose a starting ratio that you can repeat. Record dose, output, approximate time, grind position, and what the coffee tastes like. Those notes reveal more than collecting accessories.

If flow is much too fast, a finer adjustment may help; if the machine barely produces liquid, a coarser adjustment may be needed. Follow the grinder's instructions for changing settings, especially when moving finer with beans inside. Keep distribution and tamping as consistent as practical, and do not infer a grinder defect from one messy extraction through a bottomless portafilter.

What else should fit beside the machine?

Plan for the workflow around the grinder, not only its closed footprint. You need access to load beans, remove the grounds container or portafilter, clean the chute, and lift out parts for maintenance. A grinder wedged tightly beneath a cabinet may be awkward even if its listed height technically fits.

Keep a small scale and somewhere to rest the portafilter within reach. If you make filter coffee as well, decide whether you will tolerate moving between settings every day. A separate brew grinder can be a better later purchase than replacing a capable espresso grinder solely to avoid that chore.
