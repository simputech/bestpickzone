The phrase “MX keypad vs Stream Deck” can refer to two different purchases. The full MX Creative Console combines a wired LCD keypad with a separate wireless dialpad. The standalone MX Keypad is a button-focused product without that dialpad. Stream Deck MK.2: buy it for a visible command grid

The Stream Deck MK.2 is our editorial pick when discrete actions dominate: change a scene, mute an input, open a resource, execute a supported app action, or trigger a carefully configured sequence. Its LCD labels help distinguish commands that would otherwise require memorizing keyboard combinations. A button still needs the correct software action behind it; an attractive icon alone adds no capability.

For a streaming desk, inspect the integrations for your actual broadcasting and audio applications before buying. A supported plugin can expose an action more directly than a keyboard shortcut. Where you instead use a hotkey, verify application focus, shortcut conflicts, and whether the command behaves differently in another window. Neither this comparison nor a manufacturer's plugin count establishes universal compatibility.

Elgato's hardware specification lists 15 customizable LCD keys and a USB 2.0 interface. It gives dimensions of 118 × 84 × 25 mm without the stand. Those are manufacturer dimensions, not our measurements, and they do not describe the complete installed footprint with cable and stand. Check the selected retail bundle's cable type rather than assuming every listing includes the same host connector.

  • Pros: 15 visible labeled actions; software pages and folders; an established plugin marketplace that can be checked before purchase.
  • Cons: no physical dial; setup and maintenance take time; hotkey-based actions still depend on application behavior.

MX Creative Console: buy the full kit for the dialpad

The full MX Creative Console makes the strongest case when a dial and roller are central to your work. The keypad selects actions while the separate dialpad supplies continuous input and additional buttons. Two pieces let you arrange the controls independently, but they also require two useful positions on your desk. Check that both are included in the selected listing.

For photo work, ask which exact adjustment your preferred app exposes through its integration. A dial can be appealing for repeated adjustment, but we cannot claim that every slider is supported or that it improves editing quality. For video work, check timeline navigation and the particular commands you need. An integration's existence does not prove every function you use is available in your installed version.

The current Logitech product material extends beyond Adobe. Its listed integrations include creative applications such as Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve, and Figma, as well as collaboration and media tools. Availability varies by platform and plugin. The earlier idea that this is only an Adobe accessory is therefore too narrow. Conversely, a general keyboard-shortcut profile is not equivalent to a deep application integration.

The keypad uses a wired USB connection; the dialpad is wireless, with Bluetooth and compatible Logi Bolt connectivity described by Logitech. Do not assume a Bolt receiver is in every bundle. Check the package contents, spare ports, and your computer's connection options. On an employer-managed computer, confirm that Options+ and needed plugins can be installed before purchasing.

  • Pros: separate continuous controls; flexible positioning; app-specific actions through Options+ and supported integrations.
  • Cons: fewer LCD actions visible at once; two desk components; value depends heavily on the functions exposed by your apps.

Skip the full console if you only want labeled shortcut buttons. The dialpad is the reason to consider the larger kit, not a bonus that automatically justifies its price. Do not pay for controls you cannot map to a recurring task.
