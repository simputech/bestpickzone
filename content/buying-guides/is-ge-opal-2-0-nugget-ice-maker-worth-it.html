The GE Profile Opal 2.0 can be worth considering if you specifically want nugget ice often enough to justify a dedicated appliance, counter space and recurring cleaning. It is a weaker purchase if you simply need occasional cold drinks or want maintenance-free ice. This is a research-based buying assessment, not a hands-on review. We have not measured its output, noise, reliability or electricity use.

Start with the exact model: XPIO13SCSS

This guide addresses the GE Profile Opal 2.0 with Side Tank, XPIO13SCSS. On October 28, 2026, GE's product page stated that this model is no longer being manufactured. That does not establish whether a retailer has remaining inventory. Opal 2.0, Opal 2.0 Ultra and packages without a side tank are not interchangeable names. A comparison with another machine needs its own exact specifications. Compare complete model numbers and included accessories instead of assuming a newer-looking listing supplies more ice or requires less maintenance.

Counter dimensions are only the starting point

GE lists the XPIO13SCSS dimensions as 16.5 inches high, 13.43 inches wide and 17.5 inches deep. Do not use that rectangle as the complete installation envelope. Account separately for the side tank, ventilation, electrical connection, opening the ice drawer and reaching components for maintenance. Check the model's support page and owner's manual before choosing a location.

A useful planning exercise is to mark the appliance footprint with paper, then simulate reaching the tank and removing the drawer. Ask where draining and rinsing will happen. A unit that fits beneath a cupboard can still be inconvenient if every cleaning session requires moving several other appliances. Do not enclose it in a cabinet or improvise a built-in installation from a countertop photograph.

The linked manual specifies indoor household use. A patio drinks station is not automatically an approved operating location. If you entertain outdoors, plan how to serve prepared ice without assuming that the machine itself belongs beside the grill.

Separate making ice, holding ice and filling a glass

Daily production, bin capacity and first-ice time answer different questions. A large daily figure does not promise that amount will be waiting for guests at once. We have not independently verified output under different room temperatures, water conditions or patterns of use, so this guide does not present a tested pounds-per-day result or a guaranteed serving count.

List the times you actually want ice. A few drinks spread through the day differ from filling many cups together. If a gathering is your main reason for buying, calculate the ready supply you need and compare it with the exact model's storage and operating instructions. Do not assume that an ice-maker bin provides the same long-term storage as a freezer.

The side tank is a water-supply accessory, not an extra ice bin. Consider it in terms of refilling convenience and space. It does not establish that this package has a larger ice-storage compartment than another model, nor does it remove the need to inspect, clean and refill the system.

Cleaning is part of the product, not an optional add-on

Read GE's cleaning and descaling guidance for models without the large round front button before ordering. The support page separates exterior care, ice-bin care, reservoir cleaning and mineral removal. Water hardness and use affect the maintenance burden; one generic “occasional clean” description is not adequate.

The current support article and the multi-model manual linked by GE do not express every maintenance interval identically. We are therefore not publishing a universal schedule or mixing their chemical procedures. Match your model and control layout to its supplied instructions, and ask GE to resolve any conflict. Follow each complete cleaning, draining and rinsing procedure; do not combine cleaning chemicals or improvise concentrations from a buying guide.

Before purchasing, decide whether you have a practical place to drain and rinse the machine and a way to remember the required care. If that sounds like an unwanted recurring task, a lower purchase price does not fix the mismatch. Distilled water or a filter should not be treated as permission to skip hygiene instructions.

Noise: allow for a working appliance

GE's normal-sounds guidance describes sounds from the compressor, pump, fan, refrigerant and falling ice. It also distinguishes continuous grinding as a reason to contact support. This is manufacturer troubleshooting information, not our measured noise test.

We cannot rank the Opal as quiet, promise it will be unobtrusive during calls or compare its decibels with an unidentified competitor. Think about the proposed location relative to a desk, television and sleeping area. A buyer particularly sensitive to appliance sound should check the seller's return conditions before opening or using the unit, including any costs or exclusions.

Compare the full ownership cost

Compare the actual checkout total, included tank and accessories, condition, delivery, return terms and applicable warranty. Add the cleaning supplies and any model-approved consumables you expect to use. We have not measured electricity or water consumption and cannot calculate an evidence-based annual running cost from this review.

A hypothetical household spending $10 a week on purchased ice would spend $520 across 52 weeks. That arithmetic is only a budgeting example, not a claim about typical spending or Opal savings: appliance cost, utilities, cleaning, repairs and changes in use would still matter. Use your own receipts and habits rather than treating this as a payback promise.

For used or refurbished units, ask about condition, cleaning history, included components and the actual warranty offered for that sale. The manufacturer's discontinued status makes those questions useful; it does not by itself prove poor reliability or lack of support. Avoid paying a premium based on a model name that the listing does not clearly identify.

The decision to make before shopping

Choose this type of appliance for a recurring preference for nugget ice, with space and maintenance already planned. Skip or postpone it when occasional use, uncertain placement or an unwanted cleaning routine outweigh that preference. Compare any alternative by its complete model number and documented ice type; a generic inexpensive countertop ice maker is not automatically an equivalent substitute.
