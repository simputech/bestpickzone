Buy college tech in this order: a computer that meets your department's requirements, a workable charging and backup plan, then accessories for tasks you actually do. A tablet, e-reader, printer or second screen is optional unless a course or accessibility need makes it necessary. This is a researched buying guide, not a four-year durability test. We have not measured student productivity, battery endurance or savings from these products.

The course requirements come before the brand

Read your department's current hardware and software requirements before choosing a laptop. Check operating system, processor architecture, required applications, memory, storage, graphics and assessment software. A computer that runs a browser well may still be unsuitable for a particular engineering, design or laboratory course. Ask the department about supported configurations rather than assuming remote access or emulation will solve a mismatch.

Keep a suitable existing laptop if it meets those needs. Compare the total cost of a replacement, including necessary adapters, software, repair options and any required accessories. A claim that one model will comfortably last every student four years cannot be established from a specification sheet.

MacBook Air M4: an exact example, not a universal default

The 13-inch MacBook Air with M4, introduced in 2025, has a 13.6-inch display, two Thunderbolt 4 ports and MagSafe charging. Apple's specification lists configurations with different memory, storage and GPU options. Compare the full configuration, condition and seller terms; “M4 Air” alone does not identify an equivalent offer.

It is worth considering when the required software and peripherals are supported on that Mac. It is not a blanket recommendation for every major or the latest model by implication. We have not tested your applications, an all-day campus workload or a future support period. For Windows-specific requirements, choose a supported configuration rather than buying a Mac first and assuming a workaround later.

Search Amazon for the 13-inch MacBook Air M4. Check RAM, SSD capacity, keyboard layout, condition and included power equipment. Compare any older-generation offer with current alternatives at the actual total price.

iPad A16: match the Pencil precisely

For the iPad A16, Apple's compatibility list names Apple Pencil USB-C and Apple Pencil first generation. It does not list Apple Pencil Pro or second generation as compatible with this iPad. First-generation pairing and charging require the appropriate USB-C to Apple Pencil adapter, as Apple's adapter page explains.

Stylus compatibility therefore needs the complete product name. Check the exact Pencil features you need, the note app, storage and export workflow before buying a tablet bundle. The stylus and adapter may be separate purchases. A tablet can be useful for annotating lecture slides, but it does not automatically replace required desktop software or every paper workflow.

Search Amazon for the iPad A16. Read the exact device name and selected configuration. Try annotating a representative document before deciding that digital handwriting is worth the additional device and accessories.

Samsung T7: storage is only one part of backup

The Samsung T7 1 TB, MU-PC1T0T/AM, is a USB 3.2 Gen 2 portable SSD. T7, T7 Touch and T7 Shield are different variants. Do not transfer another variant's ruggedness or security features to the standard T7.

A separate drive can support backups or transport large files, but buying it does not create a backup automatically. Choose the backup software and coverage, schedule copies according to how much work you can afford to lose, and verify that a sample file can be restored. A monthly schedule is not a universal safeguard for assignments changing daily.

Apple's Time Machine instructions explain one supported Mac backup workflow. For another operating system, use its supported tools and your institution's guidance. Cloud synchronization and recoverable backup are different questions: check version history, retention, offline files and what happens after deletion. A drive kept beside the laptop also shares some physical risks.

Search Amazon for the Samsung T7 1 TB. Choose capacity for the files and backup history you need, and check the host port and cable. No drop immunity, guaranteed recovery or tested transfer rate is claimed here.

Charging: wattage, ports and cables all matter

Distinguish charging devices one at a time from powering several simultaneously. The Anker Nano II 65W A2663 manual concerns a specific charger. Do not confuse it with other Nano II products that have different port counts or power allocation.

For any replacement charger, check the device maker's requirements, supported charging protocol, cable rating and regional plug. A headline total wattage does not establish the power available to each device when several ports are used. A laptop may charge slowly or not keep up with use if the actual supplied power is inadequate.

Keep using the supplied charger when it meets your needs. A second charger may reduce daily packing, but it is optional. For power banks, identify the exact model and check the manufacturer's current safety notices before purchase; a generic brand and capacity recommendation is not enough to establish suitability.

Hubs and portable monitors: follow the connection path

USB-C describes a connector, not a guarantee that every port carries video or accepts charging. For a hub, list the actual required connections: a classroom's HDMI input, a USB-A device, a card reader or Ethernet. Check the laptop port's capabilities and the hub's documented limits together. “Seven-in-one” does not identify a unique Anker product or its display support.

A portable monitor may need separate power when the host connection cannot supply both video and sufficient power. Check the exact monitor's inputs, cable and stand rather than assuming every 15.6-inch display works through one cable. We have not measured a study-speed benefit from a second screen.

For a fixed desk needing several displays and peripherals, our dock comparison explains model-specific connection paths. A smaller adapter may be enough for a student who only needs occasional classroom HDMI.

Audio, reading and shared equipment are conditional

Choose headphones or earbuds by fit, the phone and computer you use, connection modes and the actual need for calls or listening. “AirPods Pro” without a generation and case type is not a precise recommendation. An e-reader also needs a content check: confirm the required textbook format, library lending support and whether complex PDFs work acceptably on the actual device.

Check campus printing allowances and network rules before buying a printer. Compare toner, drum and paper costs for a specific model with your own page volume; there is no verified one-semester payback here. Wireless printers and streaming devices may require campus registration or may be unsuitable for the network. Do not buy first and assume any dorm Wi-Fi will work.

A tracker, watch, webcam or mechanical keyboard is a later decision. Verify the exact phone ecosystem, app support and relevant rules, including assessment restrictions. Test your existing webcam with appropriate lighting before replacing it. No accessory is automatically a necessary gift for every student.

Spend on the supported workflow

For a browser-focused course, a suitable existing computer, dependable charging and verified backups may be enough. For media work, storage and required software may deserve more of the budget. For handwritten annotation, a compatible tablet-and-stylus package may be useful. Start with that actual workload and leave room to learn what the semester requires.
