Check what “completed” actually covers

A finished main series, a completed arc and a large backlist are not the same thing. This matters when you are buying several books because you want a known stopping point. Alex Verus: a finished main sequence with optional side stories

Benedict Jacka's series page states that the main sequence runs from Fated in 2012 to Risen in 2021, with twelve novels. Alex is a London magic-shop owner whose ability involves seeing possible short-term futures. His troubled relationship with other mages provides a premise to consider if you want magical power constrained by conflict and consequences.

This is our clearest recommendation when a declared ending is the deciding requirement. The author separately identifies novellas set in the same world; their existence does not make the twelve-novel story unfinished. At the same time, “complete main series” should not be used to sell a bundle containing only a few early books.

Use the author's first-chapter extracts to sample the voice before buying a set. If you are collecting matching editions, compare the regional publisher and format across all twelve titles. A finished story can still have uneven marketplace stock in a particular binding; completion and availability answer different questions.

Kate Daniels: a completed arc, with more Kate afterward

Ilona Andrews' reading-order page identifies Magic Bites as book one and Magic Triumphs as book ten. It then explicitly separates the Wilmington Years, explaining that the Roland arc has ended while the adventure continues. Magic Tides and Magic Claims follow as books eleven and twelve.

Choose this route if a substantial completed arc is enough for your reading plan. The premise combines mercenary Kate's work with a world in which magic and technology alternate in influence. If your requirement is that the protagonist has no later adventures, this is not the right completion claim to rely on.

The author list also includes novellas and short fiction. Decide whether you want the main novels first or a more detailed chronological route, then follow that specific list consistently. Mixing every decimal-numbered item into a shopping cart before trying book one can make a simple starting decision unnecessarily expensive.

Fever: identify the eleven-book sequence

Moning's Darkfever page introduces MacKayla Lane traveling to Ireland after her sister's murder and discovering a dangerous fae world. The author numbers Darkfever as book one. Consider it if you want that investigation alongside the series' explicitly sexual and threatening supernatural premise; do not treat “fantasy romance” as a guarantee of gentle content.

The author's Kingdom of Shadow and Light page identifies book eleven and calls it Mac's final journey. That is the scope of the conclusion we can support. It is more precise than promising that no future work will ever appear or that every reader will feel all questions are resolved.

The numbered list includes Iced, Burned, Feverborn, Feversong and High Voltage after Shadowfever. A five-book early bundle therefore does not contain all eleven numbered novels. Check actual titles and formats, not a seller's broad “collection” label.

The Others: a defined reading sequence, not a closed-world promise

Anne Bishop's recommended order lists Written in Red, Murder of Crows, Vision in Silver, Marked in Flesh and Etched in Bone, then separately lists other novels in the world. Her current FAQ says she does not know whether she will write more Others books. We therefore do not label the whole setting permanently complete.

This is worth considering if you are comfortable following a defined five-book sequence and deciding afterward whether to explore related stories. If your purchase depends on an explicit declaration that all main work is finished, use the clearer Alex Verus option instead of converting an uncertain future into a promise.

The author's book list identifies Crowbones as a sequel to Lake Silence. That is useful when buying beyond the initial sequence: “also in the world” does not mean every later book can be placed arbitrarily. Check relationships before choosing by cover or publication year.

Two ongoing series to distinguish from completed sequences

October Daye: Seanan McGuire's current catalog lists A Divided Duty as the twentieth title with a September 29, 2026 release date. It begins with Rosemary and Rue and follows a changeling connected to San Francisco and Faerie. The introductory count on the author page lags behind its numbered list, so we use the explicit titles and dates rather than repeat the older count. No completed-series claim is supported by that catalog.

The Hollows: Kim Harrison's reading order, revised August 13, 2026, continues after The Witch With No Name through American Demon and subsequent books, including Absolute Demon. An old recommendation that stopped at an earlier ending is not enough to call the current series complete. The list starts with Dead Witch Walking and separates short fiction and a prequel.

Both may still interest you if a long backlist is the real attraction. A long backlist is a different reason to choose them from a finished main sequence. Do not purchase a large set on the assumption that its last included volume is the author's last book.

Plan a manageable first purchase

Decide whether you need a finished main sequence, a finished arc or simply several books already available. Then sample the first novel, check your library and compare editions in your preferred format. Audio adaptations, dramatizations, novellas and main novels can be counted differently in retail search results. A title-by-title contents list is more useful than a large number on a bundle.
