Start with tools you can use outside the basket: an instant-read food thermometer, suitable tongs and a cleaning routine your air-fryer manual permits. Add an insert only for a specific recipe and an explicitly compatible machine. A liner is not a universal first purchase, and a rack does not automatically increase usable cooking capacity. We have not tested these accessories or measured their effect on cooking speed, crispness, cleanup or appliance life.

Which purchase solves your actual problem?

The useful starting question is what goes wrong during an ordinary meal. If you cannot tell whether chicken is cooked through, a liner is not the relevant purchase. If food is difficult to turn, look at utensils before adding another cooking level. If a small basket is routinely overcrowded, rearranging the load or cooking separate batches may be more appropriate than buying a rack that leaves less space around the food.

Why is a thermometer the first tool to consider?

Food color and an appliance timer do not establish a safe internal temperature. FoodSafety.gov recommends using a food thermometer and provides different temperatures and, where applicable, rest periods for different foods. Its current chart lists 165°F for poultry and leftovers, 160°F for ground meats, and 145°F with a three-minute rest for specified whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb. Consult the complete chart for the food you are preparing; do not apply one number to every recipe.

The manufacturer's current TP19H page identifies the product as TempPro TempSwift PLUS TP19H and lists a folding probe, rotating backlit display, temperature lock and calibration function. Older listings may still use ThermoPro branding. We are not assuming that every historical TP19H offer has identical response time or packaging, and we have not independently checked accuracy.

For a buyer, the important workflow is taking the food out of the running appliance as directed, reaching the relevant part of the food with the probe and reading the result comfortably. This handheld model is not a recommendation to leave its body inside a hot air fryer. Follow the thermometer's own use, cleaning and calibration instructions. Keep the probe clean between checks, and avoid transferring contamination from raw food to cooked food.

Consider it if you do not already have an appropriate food thermometer. Skip a duplicate if your existing tool does the job and is working correctly. Choose a readable, usable tool over a claim about a fraction of a second that you cannot independently assess.

Which tongs make sense for a coated basket?

OXO's 9-inch Tongs With Silicone Heads are a specific option to compare: OXO describes silicone contact surfaces for nonstick cookware and a locking mechanism for storage. That design makes them a reasonable shortlist item when your appliance permits silicone utensils. It does not establish that they fit every narrow basket or that a coating can never be damaged.

Check whether the jaws open enough to grip the food without squeezing it against the basket. Longer tongs may provide reach but can feel awkward in a compact drawer. A shorter pair may be easier to control yet place your hand closer to hot surfaces. Try the movement with an empty, cold appliance if possible. Choose around the food you cook rather than ordering the longest pair because it appears more substantial.

Consider them if your existing utensils are unsuitable for the coating or difficult to control. Skip them if a suitable pair is already in your drawer. Tongs are for handling food; they are not a substitute for the appliance handle or appropriate protection when moving a hot baking vessel.

Should you buy silicone or parchment liners?

Read the exact air-fryer manual before buying either. Philips currently advises against baking paper and foil in the models covered by its support article: covering the basket or collection pan can interfere with airflow, and loose material can reach the heater and burn. That guidance is a reason to reject blanket “works in every air fryer” advice, not permission to infer the rules for a different brand.

A silicone liner also requires an explicit material, temperature and fit check. A seller's basket-volume claim does not establish the shape of your basket, space around an insert or clearance above the food. We do not recommend an unidentified liner as the best accessory overall. If your manual prohibits it, cleaning convenience does not override that instruction.

Before spending money to simplify cleanup, identify what is taking time. Food trapped in a removable crisper plate may call for the manufacturer's permitted soaking and cleaning method. Buying an insert merely introduces another surface to wash unless it solves that specific problem. We have no measured cleanup savings to promise.

When are racks, pans and muffin cups worth considering?

A baking vessel can be useful for a recipe that needs containment. Philips' baking-tin guidance permits suitable ovenproof materials for the listed machines, calls for space around the vessel, and warns that the vessel and basket become hot. Use your own machine's instructions for approved dimensions and placement. Do not turn those recommendations into a universal clearance number.

Measure the internal usable space, including tapered sides, handles and the height left above a filled pan. An insert that fits into a cold drawer can still be awkward to remove when hot. A deeper pan may also leave less room for the recipe to rise. Plan both the cooking position and a stable place to put the vessel after removal.

For racks and skewer sets, check the exact model compatibility and permitted loading arrangement. More metal surface does not establish a larger evenly cooked batch. A rack is a recipe-specific option when the appliance maker supports that arrangement. Muffin cups and small pans should meet the same material and fit checks; a colorful multipack is not evidence of suitability.

Which of the other accessories can wait?

An oil sprayer or basting brush is optional if you already have a practical way to apply the amount of oil called for in your recipe and allowed by the appliance maker. We have not compared spray patterns or proved that one bottle browns food more evenly. Do not spray toward a running heater or infer that every aerosol product is permitted.

A generic mesh splatter screen is not an approved air-fryer insert or vent cover. Keep a stovetop screen for its intended task unless your appliance instructions explicitly support another use. A magnetic cooking chart can organize starting settings, but time charts cannot replace a food-temperature check. A cookbook earns space only if its recipes suit your machine and the meals you want to make.

For cleaning, use the maker's permitted tools after the appliance has cooled and been handled as instructed. “Soft brush” is not a guarantee against scratching every coating. For storage, a bin you already own can be enough; let cleaned parts dry before putting them away. Buying a dedicated organizer is unnecessary when two tools already have a home.

A small purchase plan

Write down one problem from your last few meals, check the manual, then buy at most the tool that addresses it. For a household that already owns appropriate tongs but guesses chicken doneness, a thermometer is the meaningful gap. For someone who reheats bread and vegetables and already owns suitable tools, there may be no first purchase at all. For a small-cake recipe, investigate one approved pan instead of an assortment of inserts.
