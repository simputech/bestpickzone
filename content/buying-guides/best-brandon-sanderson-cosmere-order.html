A practical Cosmere starting route is Mistborn: The Final Empire, the rest of the original Mistborn trilogy, then a standalone such as Warbreaker before deciding whether to begin The Stormlight Archive. That is an editorial suggestion for sampling different commitments, not a mandatory sequence. If a different premise appeals more, start there and keep the books within that series in order.

What actually needs to stay in order

Sanderson's official starting guide allows several entry points, including The Way of Kings, Elantris and The Emperor's Soul. It recommends sequential reading within a series and notes that The Alloy of Law reveals information about the original Mistborn trilogy. There is no need to read unrelated books first merely to earn permission to start Stormlight.

Our route below is designed for someone unsure how much fantasy reading they want to take on. A reader already enthusiastic about a long epic may reasonably choose a different path. Publication order can also work. The useful distinction is between a preference about where to begin and a sequel whose plot assumes the earlier installment.

Mistborn Era 1: a clearly bounded first commitment

The author's original-trilogy page identifies the sequence as The Final Empire, The Well of Ascension and The Hero of Ages. The first book combines a heist premise with an oppressive empire and a system of magical abilities. “Mistborn” can mean that first novel or the wider series, so check the contents when buying a set.

The practical advantage of this route is knowing what you are committing to: one first novel, followed by two specific sequels if you choose to continue. You can stop after sampling the first book or after the trilogy without purchasing a whole shared universe. We are not promising a particular emotional payoff or claiming that every reader will prefer it to Stormlight.

Search Amazon for Mistborn: The Final Empire editions. For a trilogy box, check all three titles rather than relying on the word “complete.”

Warbreaker and other standalone choices

The official standalone catalog includes Warbreaker, Elantris, Tress of the Emerald Sea and Yumi and the Nightmare Painter. Their premises differ, so a standalone is not simply a shorter version of whichever series you just finished. Read the description and sample of the one that interests you.

We suggest Warbreaker as a possible change of setting after the Mistborn trilogy, not as compulsory homework before Stormlight. The author's online library links an authorized full version. That gives you a way to investigate it before purchasing a preferred print or audio edition.

Tress offers a sea-journey premise; Yumi centers on two people whose contrasting worlds become connected. Choose by that kind of interest rather than trying to optimize every cross-reference. The catalog also includes later and far-future material, so “standalone” should not be read as a guarantee that every reference will be equally familiar to a newcomer.

Stormlight: follow the numbered sequence

The official Stormlight page lists these five main novels:

  1. The Way of Kings
  2. Words of Radiance
  3. Oathbringer
  4. Rhythm of War
  5. Wind and Truth

It places Edgedancer between books two and three and Dawnshard between books three and four. The same page describes Wind and Truth as the conclusion of the first arc, which is different from claiming the entire planned series is finished. Check the official catalog for additional short material if you want more than the main novels and these two novellas.

Before committing, sample The Way of Kings in the format you intend to use. Consider whether you want a long continuous project or several smaller reading choices. If the opening does not suit you, changing to another standalone is a reasonable choice; there is no purchase ladder you must complete to become a Cosmere reader.

Search Amazon for The Way of Kings editions.

Wax and Wayne: identify all four books

The official Wax and Wayne catalog lists The Alloy of Law, Shadows of Self, The Bands of Mourning and The Lost Metal, in that order. These books take place later in the Mistborn world and combine its magic with a changed technological and social setting.

Choose this branch if you want to revisit that world through a different cast and period. Read Era 1 first if preserving its discoveries matters to you. A three-book Wax and Wayne box is not the complete four-book sequence, even if the packaging looks like a finished set. Compare the included titles and the cost of any missing volume.

Use collections carefully to avoid spoilers and duplicate purchases

Arcanum Unbounded includes several shorter works, among them The Emperor's Soul, Edgedancer and Mistborn: Secret History. It is not a beginner anthology that should automatically be read straight through. Check the notices before each story and the publisher's contents list before buying a separate copy of something you may already own.

The author's Mistborn page warns that Secret History contains major original-trilogy spoilers and minor Bands of Mourning spoilers. For a conservative first-read route, wait until after The Bands of Mourning. That choice protects those discoveries; it is not a claim that other readers are wrong to choose a different rereading order.

For example, someone buying Arcanum Unbounded specifically for Edgedancer can read that story at its appropriate point and leave the rest for later. Marking the stories you have already read is more useful than buying another edition because its cover appears unfamiliar.

Check the book and format before ordering

Sanderson's bibliography separates Cosmere books from the Cytoverse and other works. Not every Sanderson title belongs in this reading order. Also distinguish The Way of Kings from The Way of Kings Prime; the online library lists the latter separately, and it should not replace the main series opener in a shopping list.

Some editions divide a long novel into parts. Compare the ISBN, language and contents so a purchase described as “Part One” is not mistaken for the entire novel. For audio, compare the selected recording's narrator, abridgment and part structure. A low displayed price for one part is not the total cost of the complete book.
