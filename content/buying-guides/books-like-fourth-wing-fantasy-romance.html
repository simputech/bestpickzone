The Serpent and the Wings of Night: choose the competition

Bramble's description identifies Oraya as the human adopted daughter of a vampire king. She enters the Kejari tournament and forms an alliance with the dangerous rival Raihn. That is the strongest premise-based match here if surviving a competitive structure while negotiating attraction is what you want to revisit.

The comparison has a limit: a tournament is not a military academy, and vampires do not reproduce the role of dragons in Fourth Wing. Choose the book because that difference sounds interesting, not because a recommendation claims every ingredient is the same. If school routines or rider bonds were your favorite elements, sample before committing.

The Hurricane Wars: choose the opposing loyalties

Thea Guanzon's official page places Talasyn and Alaric on opposing sides of a war. Her light magic and his shadow magic create an unexpected connection, while an uneasy alliance and attraction complicate their positions. One Dark Window: choose a different source of danger

Orbit's synopsis introduces Elspeth Spindle and the Nightmare, a spirit within her mind that protects her at a cost. A meeting with a highwayman draws her into a quest involving twelve Providence Cards and a kingdom affected by dark magic. Attraction develops within that dangerous undertaking.

Choose this if you want romance connected to a magical problem but are ready to move away from academy life. The internal presence and the curse are the meaningful differences. We do not promise that the prose is easier, the plot faster or the romance more intense than Fourth Wing; those are responses a sample can help you judge for yourself.

The publisher identifies the Shepherd King as a duology, and its edition catalog names Two Twisted Crowns as the sequel. Decorative editions are separate format choices, not additional installments. From Blood and Ash: choose duty under pressure

Jennifer L. Armentrout's book page describes Poppy's restricted life as the Maiden and the arrival of Hawke, a guard whose presence challenges what she has been told to want and believe. The author identifies it as book one of Blood and Ash. Consider it if a heroine questioning an assigned role is the thread you want to follow.
