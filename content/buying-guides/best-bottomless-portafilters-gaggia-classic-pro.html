What exactly determines Classic Pro and E24 compatibility?

Basket diameter is only the first check. The portafilter’s ears must match the group’s locking geometry, and the basket rim must seal against the gasket without forcing the handle. Ear thickness, position, and engagement matter. Two products sold as 58mm can have different lugs and therefore different machine compatibility.

Identify the machine from its rating plate or manual, including the Classic Pro/Evo/E24 designation and regional model code. The current North American E24 retains a 58mm portafilter platform, but that does not certify a random aftermarket handle. Ask whether the specific selected variant fits that exact model. This guide does not extend its recommendations to every older machine carrying “Classic” branding.

Check the seller’s compatibility statement, then the basket’s rim diameter, wall geometry, height, and the handle’s retaining spring. A worn or recently replaced group gasket can change where a correctly fitting handle locks. Do not force a handle to point at an exact clock position or file its ears to make an unverified purchase fit. Leakage, poor engagement, or a basket that will not seat needs investigation before brewing.

Should you buy the handle and precision basket together?

Buy a bundle if its supplied basket matches the dose you want and you do not already own a suitable basket. Buy the handle separately if retaining your current basket helps you learn what changed. Buy a precision basket afterward when you can repeat a recipe and have a specific reason to change its capacity or geometry.

The IMS B682TH24.5M is a documented option to consider rather than an unspecified “IMS-style” basket. IMS lists a 68mm border, 60mm body, 24.5mm height, ridgeless design, and 58mm tamper size. Its listed dose capacity changes with roast: the manufacturer’s test ranges are not a universal instruction to force one mass into every coffee.

Use the dimensions to check the handle and basket seat, then establish a dose with sufficient space below the shower screen. A deeper basket may clear an open-bottom handle while still leaving too little space above an overfilled puck. Bottomless construction removes a lower obstruction; it does not raise the Gaggia’s shower screen. Adding a puck screen also consumes space, so reassess the recipe rather than squeezing it into an already full basket.

Why does a bottomless handle show channeling?

Because you can see liquid leaving the basket before it enters a spout. Uneven starts, isolated jets, and spraying can draw attention to problems in distribution, grind, or puck preparation. The handle makes the behavior visible; removing the spouts did not create a more even coffee bed.

Use what you see alongside dose, yield, time, and taste. A visually attractive stream is not a complete extraction measurement, and a single untidy shot does not prove the basket or handle is defective. Keep the basket clean, distribute consistently, tamp level, and change one variable. If the grinder has coarse adjustment jumps, the Gaggia grinder guide addresses that limitation before you spend on another basket.

Does it solve clearance and cleaning problems?

Removing the spouts can create more room for a cup and scale, while direct access to the basket underside makes inspection easier. Measure your actual arrangement: a deep basket can protrude below the handle, and a tall cup on a thick scale can still crowd the Classic’s tray. The open design also means stray jets can reach the counter.

Check lateral handle clearance as well as vertical space. A long handle needs room beside the machine as you insert and rotate it; a wall, grinder, or crowded tamping station can obstruct that movement. Remove the basket for cleaning according to the maker’s instructions and inspect the retaining spring. A loose basket that falls out when knocking the puck is a workflow problem, not a feature.
