Refresh rate is the display’s capability, not a guarantee that your laptop, adapter, cable or game will supply that many frames. A high advertised rate can coexist with an unsuitable connection or a computer that renders far fewer frames. Work backward from the computer’s exact output and the monitor’s supported timing table instead of selecting an adapter by connector shape alone.

LG 27GP850-B: flat 1440p with important port limits

The LG is worth comparing when you want a flat screen for both documents and games and its actual offer fits the budget. LG lists a stand with height, tilt and pivot adjustment, plus HDMI and DisplayPort inputs. Its USB hub does not make it a USB-C docking monitor. The previous verdict’s claim of 96W USB-C charging was wrong: this model has no USB-C input and will not charge a laptop through a single USB-C cable.

That distinction changes the whole desk purchase. A laptop may still connect through an appropriate video adapter or dock, but you must arrange charging separately. Include those costs before calling the monitor a bargain. Also check which input reaches the rate you want; LG documents different maximums for HDMI and DisplayPort. The overclocked setting is a mode to configure, not the default capability of every attached device.

We have not measured its color accuracy, motion clarity, dark-room appearance or response times. A wide-gamut specification is not a calibration report, and a quoted 1ms setting is not the same as total input latency. If color-critical work matters, look for evidence for that exact model and your required workflow rather than assuming IPS alone proves accuracy.

Dell S2722DGM: choose the curve deliberately

Dell’s curved option makes sense for someone who wants that screen shape and can position it appropriately. Its manual specifies a 1500R curve, a height-adjustable stand and the input-specific refresh limits shown above. A curve is a preference and layout consideration; it is not evidence that the monitor is the healthiest option for long workdays or automatically better for spreadsheets.

Consider how you use the screen with another person, a second monitor or straight-line design work. If you routinely shift between positions or want matching flat screens, try the shape before buying. The VA panel label also does not prove that every transition looks better or worse than an IPS alternative. The earlier article made broad color, reliability and response-time comparisons without supporting measurements; those claims are no longer used to rank it.

For an older or refurbished example, check the supplied stand, power components, panel condition and warranty provider. A photograph of the display on a height-adjustable stand does not establish that a used listing includes it. If you will use an arm, confirm the exact mounting pattern, display-only weight and desk compatibility separately.

ASUS VG249Q3A: compact high-refresh option

The ASUS is the smaller choice here. It trades the two larger models’ pixel count for a 23.8-inch 1080p screen and a listed maximum refresh rate of 180Hz. That can be a sensible combination for a smaller desk or a computer targeting high frame rates at 1080p. It is not categorically unsuitable for productivity: text size, viewing distance and the work you do matter more than a rule that everyone must buy 1440p.

Its supplied stand tilts but does not offer height adjustment. Check where the display will sit relative to your normal posture before choosing a riser or arm. ASUS lists ELMB, not the ELMB-Sync feature claimed by the older article. Do not assume blur-reduction and adaptive-sync modes can operate together without checking the manual’s supported combinations. The listed speakers are an available feature, not something we have listened to or rated.

For games, confirm the computer’s output and actual performance at the settings you intend to use. Lowering resolution or graphics settings may change the attainable frame rate, but a monitor purchase cannot guarantee a competitive advantage. For ordinary documents, choose a readable scale and comfortable arrangement rather than leaving tiny text unchanged just to maximize workspace.

Build a total-cost comparison

Write down the monitor price, delivery, any required cable or adapter, and any stand solution you actually need. As an example, a hypothetical $240 screen plus a $35 adapter and $30 riser is a $305 setup before tax. Those numbers are illustrative, not offers. Compare complete setups instead of assuming the cheapest panel leaves the most useful budget.

New, open-box and refurbished listings can all be reasonable depending on condition, seller and return terms. Neither Amazon nor eBay is automatically faster, cheaper or easier for returns. Read the individual offer, distinguish seller warranty from manufacturer warranty, and check whether return shipping is your responsibility. Inspect the panel for damage and test all needed inputs before discarding packaging.

The previous Acer SB220Q recommendation mixed a US-style model suffix with a regional specification page. We are not carrying forward its exact refresh, mounting or certification claims without a matching regional SKU. If you consider that family, obtain the full part number and its own manufacturer sheet first. A similar product name is not enough to transfer specifications.

A connection example and a first-day check

Suppose a laptop has a video-capable USB-C port but no full-size DisplayPort socket. The required path could be USB-C video output to a compatible DisplayPort cable, then the monitor’s DisplayPort input. A charge-only USB-C cable will not provide that path. If a dock sits between the two, its resolution, refresh and host requirements become another limit. Our dual-monitor dock guide explains those checks in more detail.

After connection, select the intended resolution and refresh rate in the operating system, confirm text is readable at your chosen scaling, and try sleep and wake. Check that cables have room behind the desk and that the stand leaves space for keyboard and mouse. Adjustable furniture can help you place the screen; it cannot promise prevention of neck pain. Resolve persistent discomfort by reassessing the whole workstation rather than buying a monitor on a health claim.
