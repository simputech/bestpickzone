For a short introduction to Hercule Poirot, start with The Mysterious Affair at Styles, then sample different kinds of cases before saving Curtain for the end of your chosen route. The seven-book sequence below is our editorial starter path, not a complete bibliography or a requirement to read every case in order. Publication order is also a reasonable choice; the important exceptions concern references to earlier mysteries.

Choose between a sampler and a complete reading project

A sampler suits someone deciding whether they enjoy Christie or choosing a handful of books for a gift. A complete project suits someone who wants to see the detective across the full catalog, including short stories. Neither approach is inherently smarter. Decide how much you want to commit before ordering a box set or collecting multiple editions.

The estate's official Poirot reading list distinguishes a suggested order within Poirot's lifetime from publication dates. It also identifies spoiler-sensitive connections. In particular, it advises reading Murder on the Orient Express before Cards on the Table and leaving Curtain until last if reading the full set. Use that list for the broader project; the table here is deliberately shorter.

1. The Mysterious Affair at Styles

The official story page identifies this as Christie's first novel and Poirot's debut. It centers on a poisoning at a country house, with Hastings part of the introduction. That makes it a useful starting point if you want the detective's original context rather than meeting him through a later screen adaptation.

Our reason for putting it first is orientation, not a claim that it has the most ingenious puzzle. If the setting does not appeal, one of the travel cases below can be your first book instead. Borrowing a sample or a library copy is a sensible way to find out whether the style suits you before buying several novels.

2. The Murder of Roger Ackroyd

The estate's centenary feature describes Poirot's village setting and Dr. Sheppard's role in the investigation. Our suggestion is to read the novel before browsing detailed criticism or adaptation discussions. The less you know about the construction of the mystery, the more room you leave for your own response.

This is the next choice for someone who liked the domestic setting of Styles and wants a different investigation without changing to a large travel itinerary. If you already know the ending, that does not make the book worthless: you may still want to examine how the information is presented. We are not ranking reader enjoyment or promising a particular reaction.

Search Amazon for The Murder of Roger Ackroyd editions.

3. Murder on the Orient Express

The official account places Poirot's investigation on the famous train. This is our setting-based alternative for readers who would rather start with a confined journey than a country house. It does not require purchasing the two earlier selections first.

Several adaptations exist, so check that a purchase is the novel rather than a screenplay, dramatization or adaptation-related product. If you have seen a film, choose whether you want to compare its treatment with the book or preserve a new mystery by starting elsewhere. Avoid introductions that discuss the solution before you have read the story.

Search Amazon for Murder on the Orient Express editions.

4. Death on the Nile

The estate's page confirms the Egyptian river-steamer setting and connects its detail with Christie's travels. Place it here if you want to continue with travel mysteries while changing the setting and relationships. Its inclusion is an editorial choice, not a necessary sequel to Orient Express.

For a two-book gift, these two travel cases make a coherent theme. For your own reading, you might prefer to alternate one travel case with a village mystery. The route is useful only if it helps you select a book you want to open; it should not turn a set of largely separate investigations into an obligation.

Search Amazon for Death on the Nile editions.

5. Five Little Pigs

Five Little Pigs investigates an earlier murder through recollections. The estate also notes its US title, Murder in Retrospect. That alternate title matters when shopping used books: the two names can refer to the same novel, not two separate Poirot cases.

Choose it when you want a change from following a newly discovered crime. Comparing memories is a different reading task from keeping track of travelers arriving and leaving. If you prefer immediate events to retrospective testimony, move After the Funeral ahead of it. Neither switch interferes with the purpose of this starter route.

Search Amazon for Five Little Pigs editions.

6. After the Funeral

The official synopsis introduces suspicions surrounding a death and the family gathered afterward. It earns a place in this route as a return to family relationships and inheritance after the travel and retrospective cases.

Read it if the family-group premise interests you more than another journey. For a book club, that is a clearer selection criterion than calling one novel universally better. Agree on whether discussion will include the ending, and keep introductions or reading guides with spoilers until after everyone finishes.

Search Amazon for After the Funeral editions.

7. Curtain: Poirot's Last Case

Curtain brings Poirot and Hastings back to Styles for the detective's final case. That return is the reason to save it for the end of this route. You do not need to complete every intervening book, but reading Styles first provides a useful point of comparison.

If you decide to pursue the full catalog after the first few books, simply postpone this one. Keeping it for later is a spoiler-management choice, not a rule enforced by the series. Check the title carefully because collections and similarly named unrelated books can appear in marketplace searches.

Search Amazon for Curtain editions.

Choose the edition as carefully as the order

Compare language, format, print size and whether an audiobook is unabridged. A new cover or film tie-in does not necessarily mean new story content. For collections, compare the contents against books you already own before ordering.
