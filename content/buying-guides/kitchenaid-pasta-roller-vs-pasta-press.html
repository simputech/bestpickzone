Choose a KitchenAid pasta roller if you want lasagna sheets, filled pasta, or cut noodles. Choose the KSMPEXTA Gourmet Pasta Press if you specifically want extruded shapes such as rigatoni, macaroni, or bucatini. For a first attachment, our editorial recommendation is the roller: it lets you see and adjust the sheet before cutting. What does each KitchenAid pasta attachment actually make?

The roller flattens prepared pasta dough; the press forces prepared dough through a shaping disc. That difference matters more than the number of pieces in a bundle. A roller-only purchase needs a knife or separate cutter for noodles. A press needs its matching discs and wire cutter, but never a sheet-rolling stage.

Who should buy the roller and cutter set?

The KitchenAid KSMPRA suits cooks who want a predictable route from one batch of dough to lasagna, fettuccine, or spaghetti. Its sheet stage is also useful when you want to cut a different width by hand. You can choose the sheet before committing the whole batch to one noodle shape.

In a rolling session, divide and flatten the prepared dough, feed manageable portions through the widest setting, and progressively thin them following KitchenAid's instructions. Keep unprocessed portions covered, support the emerging sheet without pulling it, and arrange space for finished sheets. The motor frees your cranking hand; it does not eliminate the need to guide dough or organize the counter.

For ravioli, the useful purchase is the roller function. You still make the filling, portion it, seal the edges, and cut the individual pieces. If those are your intended tasks, paying for two motorized noodle cutters may add little. The KSMPSA roller alone is a sensible alternative when its current price is lower and you already like hand-cutting ribbons. Compare actual bundle prices: a discounted set can make the separate roller poor value.

  • Pros: visible control over sheet thickness; practical for filled-pasta preparation; separate cutters in KSMPRA produce two named noodle styles.

Who should buy the Gourmet Pasta Press?

The KSMPEXTA press is the more appropriate tool when the shape is the project. KitchenAid supplies six discs and a built-in wire cutter. You select a disc, feed suitable prepared dough, and cut the emerging pasta to length. You do not buy this attachment merely to avoid rolling spaghetti.

The important work shifts from managing sheets to managing extrusion. Leave room beneath the outlet for the pasta, cut at a repeatable length, and separate the pieces before they stick together. For a first session, choose one shape and follow the press manual's recipe and operating instructions. Changing shape halfway through introduces another variable while you are still learning how the dough should behave.

Do not assume a dough that rolls beautifully will extrude equally well. The pressure path and the opening in each disc differ from a pair of rollers. Start with KitchenAid's press guidance rather than transferring an internet recipe unchanged, and adjust only as its instructions permit. We have not measured output speed, shape consistency, or leftover dough, so this comparison makes no claim that one attachment produces more servings per minute.

  • Pros: makes hollow and shaped pasta unavailable from the sheet roller; interchangeable discs; wire cutter lets you choose length.
  • Cons: more small components to clean and retain; dough must suit extrusion; receiving and separating pasta remain manual jobs.

Skip this if you want lasagna or filled pasta, dislike cleaning small openings, or expect a fully automatic appliance that weighs, mixes, and extrudes for you.

Which cleaning routine and storage burden can you live with?

The traditional roller and cutters must not be immersed or put in a dishwasher. KitchenAid's manual describes dry cleaning after dough has dried. The press has a mixed cleaning routine: its housing, cutter, and plates stay out of the dishwasher and are not immersed; the auger, pasta ring, and combination tool have separate washing instructions.

That distinction prevents an expensive mistake. “Some parts dishwasher safe” does not mean the entire KSMPEXTA can go in a basket. Follow the current manual supplied with your exact model and use the provided cleaning tool where specified. Unplug the mixer before removal or cleaning. A newer 3-in-1 attachment may have a different access arrangement, so do not apply disassembly instructions from the three-piece set to it.

Storage favors different kitchens. Separate rollers can be stored individually but create several heavy pieces to locate. The press adds discs and internal parts that need an organized container. Keep the model's instructions with those parts, especially if pasta making is occasional. Neither option removes the need for a clean surface or tray for the pasta itself; owning a drying rack is not a prerequisite for choosing between these attachments.
