Choose Pachinko for a family story spanning generations, Wolf Hall for political maneuvering around Thomas Cromwell, or The Pillars of the Earth for a long medieval building-and-community story. The Nightingale and The Alice Network offer different routes into wartime fiction. This is a selection of established books to consider in 2026, not a list of novels first published this year or a claim that one is right for most readers.

Choose the scale of story before the era

A historical setting can support several kinds of novel. You might want one person's place at court, a family's changing circumstances, a town shaped by a building project, or people making decisions during occupation and war. Choosing that structure first gives you a better starting point than treating every historical novel as the same experience in different costumes.

Author and publisher sources support their identities and settings; the matching suggestions are editorial judgments about those descriptions. We have not tested reader reactions or established a universal winner. Nor should a fictional scene be treated as an independently verified historical account merely because the surrounding period is real.

Wolf Hall: choose political relationships and a central figure

Macmillan's synopsis places Thomas Cromwell amid Henry VIII's effort to end his marriage and marry Anne Boleyn. That conflict gives a concrete reason to select the book: you want a novel about influence, ambition and the workings of a court. An interest in those relationships is more useful than buying it because a list calls it the literary summit.

The publisher's trilogy page identifies the wider sequence, with Bring Up the Bodies and The Mirror and the Light following Wolf Hall. Start with the first novel if you want to follow that project in order. There is no need to buy all three before deciding whether the narrative style suits you.

Sample the prose in your preferred format. If you are shopping after watching a screen version, check that the listing contains the novel: Macmillan also publishes a stage adaptation. A related title and familiar cover art do not make the script interchangeable with the book.

Pachinko: choose the family across generations

Hachette describes Pachinko through four generations of a Korean immigrant family in twentieth-century Japan, beginning with Sunja's circumstances in Korea. That family structure makes it the option to investigate when you want to follow the consequences of decisions across more than one lifetime.

Choose it for that scope rather than a promise that it is easy for everyone or more emotionally effective than another novel. A reader looking for one contained investigation may prefer a different structure. Someone interested in migration, belonging and how family circumstances change over time has a clear reason to try a sample.

The author's publisher-hosted interview and reading guide discusses research and the writing process. It can support a discussion after reading, but the questions may reveal developments. Save them until the group has finished if preserving discoveries matters. Historical curiosity can lead to further nonfiction reading without assuming the novel represents every person's experience.

The Pillars of the Earth: plan for a long project

The publisher's description centers the building of a cathedral in twelfth-century England, involving a prior, a mason and competing personal and institutional interests. Choose this if the project and the surrounding community sound more appealing than concentrating on one court figure or a twentieth-century family.

The practical tradeoff is length. The selected publisher record includes a 992-page hardcover and a 1,008-page paperback. Those are edition-specific figures, not a single universal page count. Neither proves how quickly you will read the novel. Compare the physical format with where you will use it; an ebook or library audio copy may be easier to carry than a large hardcover.

The same page lists audio recordings of substantially different lengths. Confirm abridged or unabridged status and the selected narrator before comparing prices. The author's catalog places the novel within the Kingsbridge books, including a prequel. A chronological prequel does not mean you must purchase it before sampling the originally published entry.

The Nightingale: follow two sisters in wartime France

Macmillan describes The Nightingale through two sisters taking different paths in German-occupied France during World War II. Consider it when family relationships under occupation are the story you want to investigate.

This is not a guarantee of an easy or uplifting reading experience. A wartime family premise can involve distressing material, and a popular recommendation cannot establish your personal response. For a gift or book club, ask whether the setting and themes are wanted rather than promising that every reader will connect with it.

Check the exact edition when shopping around a screen adaptation or reissue. A future on-sale date attached to one tie-in format does not mean the original novel is unavailable in every format. Match title, author, language and publication record before deciding whether a displayed preorder is the edition you actually need.

The Alice Network: choose the linked timelines

Kate Quinn's official book description links a woman recruited into a spy network during World War I with an American woman's search for her cousin in 1947. That combination makes it the shortlist's most direct choice for someone wanting espionage alongside a later search story.

The useful distinction is structural: you are following events in two periods and learning how they connect. It is not evidence that this book reads faster than Mantel or produces a stronger emotional payoff than Hannah. If switching timelines distracts you, compare a sample with the more concentrated court setting of Wolf Hall before choosing.

The novel draws on a real network, but that does not make every character, conversation or scene a historical record. Read the author's notes for the boundary between invention and research. For a discussion group, separate what the novel depicts from what you want to investigate through historical sources afterward.

Make the edition and reading plan work together

Before buying a collection, compare its contents with books you already own. For a group, agree on language and any translation or format requirements, then allow enough reading time for the selected edition. An audiobook sample helps you assess narration; a print sample helps with type size. Borrowing the first choice is a useful way to decide whether to purchase a copy or continue a series.
