Mark Manson Books: What to Read First and What to Skip

By BestPickZone Editorial Team · Updated September 30, 2026

Start with The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck if you want the clearest introduction to Mark Manson’s approach to priorities and personal responsibility. Choose Models for its specific dating focus, Everything Is F*cked for questions about hope, or Will if you would rather follow a life story. Those are different purchases, not interchangeable doses of motivation.

This guide to Mark Manson books separates the main reading choices from the companion journal and explains a sensible reading order. Recommendations are editorial judgments based on the books’ stated scope, author information, and verified editions. We do not promise that a book will change your life, diagnose a problem, or suit every reader who likes blunt advice.

Which Mark Manson book should you read first?

For most first-time readers, The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck is the best starting point. Its central concern is selection: deciding which difficulties and commitments deserve your energy. The provocative title can obscure that emphasis. The book is concerned with caring deliberately, rather than treating indifference as a personality.

That distinction is useful if you arrive expecting permission to neglect obligations. Consider two possible goals: impressing everyone at work and doing a particular job well. The first gives other people permanent control over the outcome; the second leaves room for decisions you can actually make. This is our example of the kind of values question the book invites, not a claim that reading it guarantees better work or relationships.

The recommendation is strongest for someone who keeps chasing the next external sign that life is going correctly. It is weaker for someone who already knows what matters and needs an implementation plan. If your problem is remembering a routine or arranging your environment, start with our James Clear book guide instead of expecting Manson to supply the same kind of system.

The tone also belongs in the buying decision. Profanity and confrontation are part of the delivery, so sample a chapter before gifting it to someone who prefers gentle encouragement. The author’s official book page offers background and a sample option. A familiar orange cover is not enough reason to assume the book fits a particular person.

Choose it for: clarifying priorities and accepting tradeoffs. Skip it if: you want a quiet, structured workbook or dislike abrasive humor.

What does Everything Is F*cked add?

Everything Is F*cked: A Book About Hope shifts the emphasis from individual priorities toward the stories and expectations that make life feel meaningful. It is the more suitable follow-up if the questions that stayed with you were philosophical. It is a less obvious purchase if you mainly wanted a practical checklist.

Manson’s description of the book frames it around hope, emotion, and the tension between apparent progress and dissatisfaction. Read that as the author’s argument to consider, not as a settled explanation of everyone’s experience. The worthwhile buying question is whether you enjoy watching a writer challenge a premise before offering a different way to think about it.

There is no fictional plot that requires you to finish The Subtle Art first. We recommend that order because the narrower starting question makes it easier to decide whether Manson’s voice works for you. If it does not, buying the second book merely because its cover looks like a matching set is unlikely to resolve the mismatch.

For a book club, this is the better choice when members want to disagree productively about ideas. Ask each reader to identify one assumption they accept and one they question. For a commuter who wants one simple action to practice after every chapter, that same openness may feel frustrating. The extra conceptual range is the attraction and the limitation.

Choose it for: discussion about hope and meaning. Skip it if: your main need is a step-by-step habit plan.

Who is Models actually written for?

Models: Attract Women Through Honesty is a dating book directed at men seeking relationships with women. That narrower audience makes it a different recommendation from Manson’s general self-help books. The author’s description centers self-development and honest connection rather than rehearsed techniques for impressing someone.

A reader who is exhausted by trying to perform a supposedly attractive personality has a clear reason to consider it. The relevant question is whether you want to examine how you present yourself and handle vulnerability. It is not a promise that a particular conversation will work, and another person’s attraction is never an outcome you can purchase or control.

That audience boundary matters when recommending it as a gift. A person looking for advice about an established marriage, communication across a different relationship context, or repairing trust may need a more specifically matched book. Our relationship-book guide is a better place to compare those needs. Buying Models because it happens to be by the same author can send you toward the wrong problem.

Read it before the other books if dating is your actual concern; there is no benefit in completing an author’s catalog as homework. Conversely, leave it out of a general Manson reading plan when the topic is irrelevant. A useful author guide should make it easy to buy fewer books, including declining the one with the least connection to your life.

Choose it for: its stated focus on honest dating and self-presentation. Skip it if: you need a broad relationship reference or advice tailored to an established couple.

Why does Will belong on this list?

Will is Will Smith’s memoir, written with Mark Manson. It belongs in a guide to Manson’s work because of that collaboration, but it should never be presented as Manson’s autobiography. Smith’s life and perspective are the subject; the co-author credit explains a different part of how the book was made.

The official description traces Smith’s movement through music, film, ambition, and family life. Choose it when you prefer an extended personal narrative to a succession of arguments. A memoir lets a reader consider what a decision cost within a particular life. It does not automatically turn that decision into a universal rule for everyone else.

This is also the clearest format-specific choice here: the Amazon audiobook listing credits Smith as narrator. If hearing the memoir in its subject’s voice is your reason for choosing audio, check the narrator on the edition you select. Do not assume Manson narrates it because his name appears in the author field.

Readers looking exclusively for Manson’s direct advice should leave this until later, if they buy it at all. Readers interested in celebrity memoir can start here without any self-help prerequisite. Choose it for: a memoir about ambition and personal life. Skip it if: you want Manson speaking directly as the central authorial voice throughout.

Should you buy the journal as well?

The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck Journal is an official companion, not a replacement for the original book. The author’s page describes guided prompts organized around the books’ themes. That makes it a reasonable second purchase for someone who likes writing their way through a question.

The distinction is practical. Reading about priorities and putting your own competing commitments on paper are separate activities. If you reliably use prompts, a dedicated journal gives you a place to keep that work. If you already reflect in a notebook, you may prefer to keep using it. Owning a branded journal is not a prerequisite for applying an idea.

Buy the print edition intentionally. Inspect whether a used copy has completed pages, and confirm that the author is Mark Manson rather than a third party selling a summary or unofficial workbook. Amazon search results can mix originals, collections, and companion products. The exact title and author matter more than a similar-looking cover.

A reading order that avoids unnecessary purchases

Begin with The Subtle Art, then pause before buying anything else. Write down the question it leaves unresolved. If that question concerns meaning, move to Everything Is F*cked. If you want guided reflection, consider the journal. If you want a life story, change modes with Will. Put Models on a separate track driven by its dating subject.

For readers who want a gentler exploration of vulnerability, our comparison of Daring Greatly and The Gifts of Imperfection offers another route. You do not need to stay with one author because the first book raised a useful question. The next purchase should answer your next question.

Our final recommendation remains one book first, selected by purpose. Check the language, format, author credit, and seller before ordering.
