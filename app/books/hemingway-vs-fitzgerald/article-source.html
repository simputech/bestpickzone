Hemingway vs. Fitzgerald: Which Novel Should You Read First?

By BestPickZone Books Editors · Research checked October 17, 2026

Start with Fitzgerald's The Great Gatsby if you want a concentrated novel about wealth, longing and self-invention. Start with Hemingway's The Sun Also Rises if you want a restless social world in which dialogue and behavior reveal relationships that the characters struggle to explain. Neither choice requires a biography first. Sample the narrators before deciding which voice you want to spend time with.

Our comparison focuses on these two entry points, with Tender Is the Night as a later Fitzgerald option. It does not award a winner for prose, masculinity, influence or the authors' personal lives. Those categories can start a discussion, but they do not produce a useful shopping score. A specific book and a clear reason to read it are more practical.

What is the difference you will notice on the page?

In these novels, Fitzgerald frequently makes the description itself part of the attraction: houses, parties and gestures carry emotional meaning through Nick Carraway's telling. Hemingway's Jake Barnes often moves through conversations, meals and journeys with less direct explanation of the feelings underneath. This is an editorial comparison of the two texts, not a rule that every sentence by either author follows.

The important question is what you enjoy doing as a reader. Do you like lingering over an image and reconsidering the narrator's judgment? Or do you prefer noticing the pressure in an apparently casual exchange? Both books reward inference. Plain vocabulary does not make Hemingway's emotional situation simple, and Fitzgerald's descriptive richness does not turn Gatsby into a plotless exercise.

Why begin with The Great Gatsby?

Fitzgerald's original text gives Nick the job of telling the story of his neighbor Jay Gatsby and Gatsby's connection to Daisy Buchanan. The parties and expensive surroundings are part of the setup, but the reading interest lies in what those displays promise and what they fail to settle. You do not need to admire the characters to find their choices compelling.

For a first reading, follow Nick's position in each scene. Sometimes he observes, sometimes he participates, and sometimes he passes judgment after the fact. Ask what he directly witnessed and what reached him through another person. This gives you a concrete way into the narration without requiring a study guide or memorizing a list of symbols before the story has begun.

Our recommendation is to read the novel before an introduction that discusses the ending. Let its recurring images acquire meaning through the events around them. You can return to the critical material afterward if you want help connecting details. Buying an annotated edition is optional; it is most useful when you enjoy pausing for historical and cultural context.

Skip it as a first choice if you mainly want a glamorous love story to admire. Wealth and attraction are central, but the book puts pressure on both. If a screen version is your only reference, sample the written opening: Nick's voice is a major part of what the novel offers, and a beautiful cover or familiar film image cannot convey it.

Why begin with The Sun Also Rises?

Hemingway's original novel follows Jake Barnes and a group whose relationships shift across Paris and a trip to Spain. Lady Brett Ashley, Robert Cohn and the others bring competing attachments into meals, drinking, travel and the fiesta. Much of the interest is in the difference between what people say and how they behave toward one another.

Choose it when you enjoy reading a conversation twice to hear what has changed. A brief exchange can carry jealousy, affection or resentment without a paragraph explaining the emotion. The surface movement from place to place is therefore not the whole story. Reading only for the next major event may make the book feel less rewarding than attending to those social adjustments.

There are reasons to sample it before buying. The text includes antisemitism, racist language, heavy drinking and bullfighting. Those are substantive parts of the reading experience, not details erased by calling it a classic. An edition with contextual notes may help you discuss the portrayals, but notes do not make the content disappear.

Skip it if you want straightforward escapist travel or a comforting romantic resolution. The Paris cafés and Spanish settings should not be mistaken for a holiday recommendation. Our suggested approach is to watch who invites, excludes, interrupts or changes the subject. That makes the book's relational tension more visible without pretending there is one compulsory interpretation.

When should you move to Tender Is the Night?

Choose Tender Is the Night when you want a different Fitzgerald relationship study after Gatsby, rather than another version of Gatsby's parties. The publisher's description places actress Rosemary Hoyt and Dick and Nicole Diver in the south of France. Dick's roles as Nicole's husband and psychiatrist are central to the relationship it describes.

That is a reason to choose the book deliberately. It brings wealth, dependence and professional boundaries into a marriage-centered story. It is not our shortest path to deciding whether Fitzgerald's voice appeals to you. If Gatsby's concentration was what you liked most, sample this novel before assuming that more Fitzgerald automatically means the same experience.

Skip it for now if you want a lighter social comedy. Mental illness and unequal power are part of its subject. For an edition with a foreword or critical introduction, check whether that material gives away later developments. Read the opening fiction first if preserving the discovery matters more to you than receiving a critical framework in advance.

How should you choose an edition?

Check the full title, author, language and whether the text is complete. A simplified learner's edition, a study guide and the original novel can appear together in search results. If you want notes, look for an identified editor and a description of what the edition adds. A higher price may reflect binding, design or collectible status rather than a different reading text.

Do not use page count as the sole completeness check: type size, margins and introductions affect it. For a book club, using the same edition can make page references easier, but check that everyone is buying the same ISBN before ordering. For audio, sample the narrator and confirm abridged or unabridged status independently of a print listing.

Project Gutenberg provides the two linked entry-point texts and marks the editions as public domain in the United States; readers elsewhere should check their local terms. A free sample can be enough to choose between the voices. If you then buy print, choose for legibility and useful extras rather than a promise that one edition will make the novel effortless.

A useful two-book reading plan

Read Gatsby first if you want to track aspiration through Nick's observations; read The Sun Also Rises first if the tension of Jake's social circle appeals more. Then switch authors. Compare how each narrator describes a gathering, handles a disappointment and judges the people around him. Those questions keep the comparison connected to the novels rather than the authors' public personas.
