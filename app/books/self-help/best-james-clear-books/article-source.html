James Clear Books — Atomic Habits, the official workbook, and what to read next

Start with Atomic Habits for the framework. Consider The Atomic Habits Workbook if structured exercises will help you apply it. The seven additional books below are by other authors, chosen for different questions rather than presented as more James Clear titles.

Our earlier claim that James Clear had written only one book was out of date. His official books page now lists Atomic Habits and its companion workbook. These serve different purposes: the original explains the approach, while the workbook provides guided practice. You do not need to buy both at once. Start with the obstacle in front of you: understanding habits, putting an idea into practice, concentrating on demanding work, or deciding which commitments deserve your time.

The original book · start here

Atomic Habits

James Clear · 2018 · ~320 pages

Clear's whole argument is that you don't rise to the level of your goals; you fall to the level of your systems. The book turns that into a usable framework — make a habit obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying (and invert those four laws to break a bad one) — wrapped around the idea that lasting change is really identity change: you're not trying to read a book, you're becoming a reader.

The useful question is whether its system-based approach gives you a concrete action to try. Readers who want a compact, practical framework may prefer it to a broad survey of behavioral science. It is not a substitute for testing what fits your circumstances, and buying another book is not the same as applying the first one. See Clear's description of the original book for its intended scope.

Skip this if: you've already read a stack of habit books — you'll recognize a lot of the underlying ideas, just more cleanly packaged.

Official companion · optional practice

Should you buy The Atomic Habits Workbook?

The workbook is a sensible next purchase if you understand the original book but want prompts and templates to turn it into a personal plan. It is not a sequel you must read to finish the argument. Readers who already use their own notebook or tracker may have little reason to add it.

Avery lists the official workbook as a December 9, 2025 publication by James Clear. Its guided material covers habit tracking, habit stacking, reflection on your environment and adapting habits as circumstances change. Check the author's name and the word Workbook: third-party summaries, study guides and unofficial workbooks can appear in the same search results.

Our recommendation is to sample the format before ordering. If blank prompts make you more likely to sit down and plan, the structure may be useful. If you dislike writing in books, a writable paperback may not suit you; inspect how the format you choose handles the exercises. Do not assume an audiobook, ebook and print workbook provide the same practical experience.

Skip this if: you are looking for a different theory of behavior change, or still have not tried an idea from Atomic Habits. The companion helps with application; it does not remove the need to choose a small action and follow through.

Best for the "why"

The Power of Habit

Charles Duhigg · 2012

Reported habit stories2012

Charles Duhigg’s The Power of Habit is our follow-up for readers who want reported stories about behavior in organizations and everyday life. Duhigg’s book page describes its narrative approach to habit research. Choose it for case studies and explanation rather than expecting another version of Clear’s practical framework.

That difference can help you avoid buying overlapping advice. If examples make an idea easier for you to examine, Duhigg may be a useful next read. If you already understand the habit you want to change and need to practice, the official workbook or your own notebook may be more useful than another explanatory book. The recommendation is about reading purpose, not proof that every case study generalizes.

Skip this if: you want step-by-step instructions — this is more explanation than how-to.

Closest cousin · read next

Tiny Habits

BJ Fogg · 2020

A simpler method2020

BJ Fogg’s Tiny Habits is an alternative implementation approach for someone who wants a manageable starting action. The author’s book page presents it as a practical behavior-design method. We recommend comparing its sample with Clear’s before buying, because their subjects overlap even though they are separate authors and frameworks.

Use it when your obstacle is getting started, and read with one behavior in mind. For example, decide what a small repeatable action would look like in your own schedule, then assess whether the method helps you specify it. Do not treat a promotional success claim as a guarantee for your situation. If Clear already gives you enough structure to act, you may not need both books.

Skip this if: you felt Atomic Habits already covered this ground — there's meaningful overlap between the two.

Best for the belief layer

Mindset

Carol S. Dweck · 2006

The psychology of change2006

Carol S. Dweck’s Mindset addresses beliefs about ability and development. The publisher’s description distinguishes fixed and growth mindsets and notes later material on false growth mindset. Choose it when your question concerns how you interpret challenge and feedback, rather than how to arrange a daily reminder.

We would read it as a different lens, not as a necessary scientific foundation for Clear’s book. Beliefs, resources, opportunities and circumstances are not interchangeable, and a reading recommendation should not promise that adopting a label fixes every obstacle. Compare an updated edition’s contents if the additional discussion is your reason for choosing the book.

Skip this if: you want concrete habit tactics — this is about beliefs, not routines.

4
Best for knowledge workers

Deep Work

Cal Newport · 2016

Where to aim habits2016

Cal Newport’s Deep Work is our choice when the desired habit is protected concentration. Newport’s own bibliography identifies its focus on working without distraction and developing that skill. This makes it more specific than a general book about building routines: the central question is what conditions support demanding work.

Read it with your actual working arrangements in view. A person who controls a writing schedule faces different constraints from someone required to answer calls or serve customers. Choose one feasible change to consider instead of treating another person’s ideal schedule as a requirement. If focus is not the problem you are trying to solve, a different recommendation on this page may fit better.

Skip this if: your habits are about health or lifestyle rather than focus and output.

5
Best for the long game

Grit

Angela Duckworth · 2016

Sustained effort2016

Angela Duckworth’s Grit considers sustained interest and effort toward long-term goals. Her official book page presents the argument through passion and perseverance. We suggest it when you want to reflect on a continuing project, rather than when you need an immediate checklist for organizing tomorrow morning.

The useful buying distinction is time horizon. Clear’s routine framework and Duckworth’s discussion of sustained effort ask different questions. Read the latter as an argument to examine, not a verdict that success or difficulty reflects personal character alone. Persistence also needs a worthwhile direction; purchasing a book does not decide whether a particular goal deserves another year of your time.

Skip this if: you want a practical daily routine rather than a discussion of long-term effort.

Best deep dive · save for later

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman · 2011

Judgment and decisions2011

Daniel Kahneman’s Thinking, Fast and Slow examines judgment and decision-making through fast and deliberate modes of thinking. The publisher’s description explains that broader scope. Choose it because you want to examine how you reason, not because it is a required final volume in a habit-building course.

This is a different reading project from applying a small daily routine. We would approach it in sections and keep its questions about confidence and error separate from claims that it proves every practical technique in another author’s book. If your immediate need is a workable reminder or a less distracting desk, reading a broad account of cognition can wait.

Skip this if: you want something quick and applicable — it's long, dense and academic by design.

The original · principles over tactics

The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People

Stephen R. Covey · 1989

A different method1989

Stephen R. Covey’s The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People uses “habits” in a wider framework of personal and interpersonal principles. FranklinCovey’s overview includes priorities, responsibility and understanding others. Choose the book when the question is what deserves your effort, rather than only how to make an action repeatable.

That scope is a useful contrast to a book centered on everyday behavior. It can suit someone reassessing commitments or working relationships. It is not a promise of a scientifically measured improvement from reading seven principles, and its associated courses are separate purchases. Check that your selected listing is the book by Stephen R. Covey rather than a summary, workbook or training product.

Skip this if: you specifically want a narrow behavior-change method rather than a broader principles framework.

So what should you actually read?

▶ Start here
Atomic Habits. Start with the original framework before deciding whether the official workbook would help you apply it.
↓ Read next
The Power of Habit for the science behind it, or Tiny Habits if you want an even smaller method to put into practice.
✕ Save for last
Thinking, Fast and Slow. A landmark — but dense. Come back to it once the practical books have you wanting the deep theory.

How should you avoid buying the same advice twice?

Choose one follow-up for a specific reason. Use the official workbook for structured practice, Tiny Habits for a different implementation method, or Deep Work when the problem is protecting sustained attention. A stack of overlapping self-help books can postpone the decision to try anything. Borrow or sample a title first if you are unsure whether it adds a useful perspective.

The seven recommendations above remain separate works by their credited authors. They are not coauthored by James Clear and are not required companions to his books. For more options, browse the self-help hub or compare the distinct reader-fit advice in our Mark Manson guide.
