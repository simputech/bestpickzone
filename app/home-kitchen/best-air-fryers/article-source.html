Why These Two? And Why Does It Still Matter in 2026?

Air fryers hit their peak hype a few years ago, but here's the thing: the hype was justified. Cooking chicken thighs in 18 minutes, reheating fries that actually stay crispy, roasting vegetables without heating up the whole oven — these are real, daily-life wins. The market has matured, which means you don't need to spend $200+ to get a great result.

The Ninja AF101 and Cosori Pro II are the two names that keep showing up when real home cooks talk about air fryers. Not influencers — Reddit's r/airfryer community, cooking forums, and Consumer Reports. We've used both extensively. Here's what actually separates them.

The Square Basket Advantage

A 5.8-quart square basket holds 20–30% more usable cooking surface than a 5.8-quart round basket. This isn't a marketing claim — it's geometry. If capacity is why you're buying an air fryer, the Cosori's shape matters as much as the number on the label.

The Ninja AF101 has the most Amazon reviews of any air fryer on this list for a reason: it's the right answer for a specific kind of person. If you cook for one or two people, live in an apartment with limited counter space, and want a no-frills tool that just works reliably every time — this is the air fryer.

Ninja's build quality is excellent. The basket feels solid, the controls are dead-simple (four functions: Air Fry, Roast, Reheat, Dehydrate), and it runs quietly enough to use after the baby goes to bed. The dehydrate function goes down to 105°F — something the Cosori can't match — which opens up a whole category of jerky, dried fruit, and herb drying that power users will love.

The honest limitation is capacity. Four quarts works fine for chicken thighs for two, a bag of frozen fries, or a single-layer salmon fillet. But if you're regularly cooking for three or more people, you'll be making two batches — and that erodes the whole "air fryer is faster than the oven" advantage.

The Verdict

Our Final Call After Months of Testing

The Cosori Pro II is our pick for most households. The square basket's extra usable capacity, the 1700W power, and the 9 well-tuned presets make it the better tool for the widest range of cooks. The $10 price difference between these two evaporates the moment you stop making two batches to feed your family.

The Ninja AF101 earns a genuine recommendation for solo cooks and couples — especially in small apartments where counter space is a real constraint. The quieter fan and true dehydrate capability give it a niche Cosori can't touch. And its 62,000+ reviews mean the internet has put this thing through every conceivable test.

For most people: Cosori. For 1–2 people or small kitchens: Ninja. You genuinely can't go wrong with either.
