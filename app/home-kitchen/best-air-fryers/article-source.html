When should you choose the Cosori CP358-AF?

The Cosori CP358-AF is the larger-capacity option here. Choose it when the food you plan to cook benefits from more basket space and you prefer preset controls. Confirm the model code on the actual listing: “Cosori 5.8-quart” alone is not a sufficiently precise identity.

VeSync's official listing identifies this model as non-smart, with ten customizable presets, preheat and a shake reminder. It does not establish Wi-Fi or app control for this unit. The store showed it as sold out on October 17, 2026; this is a dated availability observation, not a promise about other sellers.

For a buyer, the useful question is whether those controls reduce friction in a familiar routine. If you regularly make the same side dish, a repeatable setting can be convenient. If you usually follow recipes with their own temperature and time, a long preset list may contribute little to your decision. Neither habit requires declaring one interface universally superior.

  • Pros: Larger stated capacity of these two models; customizable presets; reminder to check and shake food.
  • Cons: Exact-model stock needs checking; no app control; a larger capacity does not guarantee a complete family meal in one batch.

Skip this if you specifically want connected controls or cannot verify CP358-AF. Do not accept another Cosori model as an equivalent purchase merely because its basket capacity and photographs look similar.

When should you choose the Ninja AF101?

The Ninja AF101 makes sense when a four-quart basket suits your meals and you value its four named functions. Its dehydrate setting is a concrete distinction from the Cosori controls discussed here. The smaller capacity is a tradeoff to assess against your portions, not proof of inferior cooking.

The manufacturer-authored AF101 owner's guide, available in this manual archive, covers Air Fry, Roast, Reheat and Dehydrate, and lists a 120V, 1550W electrical rating. We have not performed a controlled head-to-head cooking or sound test. Wattage alone therefore does not support a claim that this model is slower, quieter or less consistent.

Think about the food that would frustrate you if it required a second batch. A small portion of leftovers is a different job from several large pieces that need to sit separately. If the latter describes most dinners, investigate basket dimensions and usable arrangement before ordering. A nominal quart figure does not tell you how your particular meal fits.

  • Pros: Clear selection of four functions; dedicated dehydrate mode; smaller capacity may suit individual portions.
  • Cons: Less stated capacity than CP358-AF; batch cooking may be necessary; similarly named Ninja models are separate purchases.

Skip this if your usual meal requires more space than a four-quart basket provides. Buying a smaller machine and consistently cooking two batches may be a poor trade even when its initial price is attractive.

How should you check capacity and counter space?

Write down three ordinary meals you want the air fryer to handle. Then decide whether you are cooking the main dish, a side or reheating leftovers. This simple distinction is more useful than an unqualified “feeds four” promise: four portions of one food do not necessarily occupy the same space as four portions of another.

Measure the intended counter location and the route for pulling out the basket. Include somewhere suitable to place hot removable parts. A machine that fits in a cupboard still needs an appropriate operating position with the ventilation clearance required by its manual. Check that location before buying rather than planning to improvise around a hot appliance.

The Cosori CP358-AF manual hosted by Home Depot lists a 170–400°F range and explains the two-basket release arrangement. Only separate its baskets while supported on a flat, heat-resistant surface. Read the supplied manual for your exact unit; photographs of a different generation are not operating instructions.

What does the Cosori recall mean for this comparison?

The CPSC's February 2023 recall lists specific Cosori model numbers, including CP158-AF and several variants. CP358-AF is not on that notice's list. That distinction is why this guide uses model codes rather than treating every Cosori 5.8-quart appliance as the same product.

Check the label on the unit against the official notice, especially for used or old-stock purchases. CPSC instructs owners of affected models to stop using them and follow the replacement process. Absence from this particular list is not a blanket safety certification or a substitute for checking current notices and the appliance's condition.

What is the sensible first purchase?

Start here: choose the exact CP358-AF if you can verify stock and want its larger stated capacity; choose AF101 if your portions fit and its controls suit your routine. If neither exact model is available from a seller you are comfortable buying from, pause the purchase and compare a currently available model on its own merits.
