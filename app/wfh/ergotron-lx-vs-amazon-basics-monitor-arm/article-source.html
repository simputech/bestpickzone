When is the original LX worth considering?

The LX is worth considering when you can match the documented part number to a complete kit and your monitor sits within the supported range. That gives you a clear basis for a fit check. It does not justify a premium for a screen that the mount manufacturer has not confirmed compatible.

The original LX 45-241-026 should arrive as an arm kit, not an extension accessory bearing the LX name. Check the package contents against the specification sheet. A replacement arm segment, wall bracket or tall-pole kit answers a different installation problem, even if the listing includes several of the same promotional images.

Choose clamp or grommet installation before ordering. A usable hole does not automatically make a grommet installation suitable: the underside must permit the required hardware and the desktop must support it. Likewise, a rear edge may have an apron or cable tray that blocks a clamp. Ask the desk and mount manufacturers about an uncertain surface instead of treating a reinforcement plate as universal approval.

How do you check the monitor before the arm?

Find the monitor's weight without its original stand, its mounting-hole pattern and any manufacturer-specific adapter requirement. Screen diagonal is only a first filter. A display can share a diagonal measurement with another screen while placing its weight differently or requiring a different bracket interface.

Write down the complete display model, including the suffix. Then check the monitor manual for stand removal and mounting requirements. Include permitted attached accessories when asking the arm manufacturer to confirm fit. Do not subtract or ignore a mounted light, adapter or other component merely because the bare panel appears under the maximum load.

For a deep curved screen, a limit based only on pounds is an incomplete answer. Ask about that exact display instead of assuming a flat-screen example applies. Also consider where the VESA holes sit on the back: the same arm lift can position two screens differently when their mounting locations differ. The intended eye-level position matters more than maximum extension in an advertisement.

Will the arm fit the room and cable route?

Map the screen position and the arm's folded space before buying. A long reach does not guarantee that the screen can sit close enough to the rear edge of a shallow desk. The wall, monitor depth, clamp location and articulated joints all affect the position you can actually use.

Mark the desired screen front edge on the desktop with removable tape. Compare that position with the manufacturer's dimensional drawing and leave space behind the desk where required. If the arm must fold sideways, check that it does not occupy a second monitor's space or block a shelf. Test the plan at both sitting and standing heights when the desk moves.

Route power and display cables with the slack specified for movement, keeping them away from pinch points. Check the full intended motion before finalizing cable ties. A cable pulled tight can become the limiting factor long before the arm reaches its mechanical range. Follow the manual's assembly and tension sequence, and stop if the screen will not hold safely within the documented setup.
