The Core Tradeoff

Same Core Function. Different Quality Ceiling.

Both desks do the fundamental thing: raise and lower electrically with a button or controller, let you sit when you want and stand when you want, and hold your monitor, keyboard, and coffee without wobbling noticeably at sitting height. The performance difference becomes most apparent at maximum standing height — the point where frame rigidity matters most and where cheaper desks reveal their wobble.

The Fully Jarvis is the right desk for most people because most people don't push standing desks to their stability limits. At sitting height and moderate standing heights, the Jarvis performs excellently and at a price that's $150–$300 less than the Uplift V2. The Uplift V2 earns its premium for taller users (who stand at higher frame extensions), heavy setups (multiple monitors, heavy equipment), and buyers who want a 15-year warranty and US-based customer support as part of the deal.

Height Range Matters More Than You Think

The Fully Jarvis tops out at 47.5" (standard). The Uplift V2 goes to 51.1". For a 6'4" user, proper standing desk height is approximately 44–48" — right at the Jarvis's ceiling. For anyone taller, or anyone who prefers anti-fatigue mat thickness under them while standing, the Uplift's extra 3.6" of range isn't a luxury — it's a requirement.

What the 15-Year Warranty Actually Means

Uplift's 15-year warranty covers the frame, motors, and electronics. Fully's 8-year warranty covers the same. In practice, most standing desk motors last well beyond either warranty period with normal use — so the warranty difference is more a signal of build confidence than a likely-to-be-used protection. If you plan to keep this desk for 10+ years and resell value matters to you, the Uplift's longer warranty period is a meaningful factor.

  • You want the bamboo top aesthetic
  • Budget is $549–$699 and you don't want to exceed it

The Verdict

Jarvis for Most People. Uplift V2 If You're Buying Once and Keeping It.

The Fully Jarvis is the right choice for the majority of buyers. At $549–$699 with the bamboo top, it performs excellently for normal standing desk use — sitting height, moderate standing height, single or dual monitors — and has the broadest community of users, reviews, and troubleshooting documentation of any desk in this category. The bamboo top option is a genuine differentiator that many find more appealing than standard laminate.

The Uplift V2 earns its premium for three specific buyer profiles: users over 6'4" who need the extra height range, anyone running a heavy dual-monitor or broadcast setup who will push the frame's stability at max height, and buyers who think of this as a furniture purchase rather than a gadget and want the 15-year warranty that reflects that. For everyone else, the $150–$300 difference is better spent elsewhere.

Most buyers: Fully Jarvis. Tall users, heavy setups, long-term buyers: Uplift V2.
