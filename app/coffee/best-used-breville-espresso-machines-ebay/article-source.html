Buying a used Breville espresso machine can make much more sense than automatically buying new—but only if you know which model fits your routine and what to inspect before paying.

eBay is especially useful for Breville machines because shoppers can compare pre-owned, open-box, refurbished, and occasionally new machines in the same search. The problem is that a cheap espresso machine is not automatically a bargain. Missing portafilters, neglected cleaning cycles, scale buildup, weak steam performance, and expensive shipping can erase the savings quickly.

This guide focuses on four Breville machines that make the most sense to look for used: the Bambino, Bambino Plus, Barista Express, and Barista Pro.

Quick answer

If you already own a capable espresso grinder, start your search with the Breville Bambino. It is compact, has no integrated grinder to evaluate, and gives you the core espresso features most beginners actually need. That simpler setup is a buying consideration, not a claim of a proven lower failure rate.

Choose the Bambino Plus if milk drinks are a priority and you value automatic milk texturing.

The Barista Express makes more sense if you want an all-in-one machine with an integrated grinder and can verify that both the brewing and grinding systems work correctly.

The Barista Pro is the more polished all-in-one option, but the used price needs to be meaningfully below new pricing before taking on the extra risk of buying secondhand.

1. Breville Bambino BES450: best used Breville for most value-focused buyers

The Bambino is the first machine I would search for if the goal is getting into real espresso without buying an oversized appliance.

Breville builds the Bambino around a 54mm portafilter, PID temperature control, low-pressure pre-infusion followed by 9-bar extraction, and its ThermoJet heating system. Breville says the machine reaches operating temperature in about three seconds. See Breville's Bambino specifications.

More importantly for a used buyer, there is no built-in grinder.

That matters because an all-in-one machine has two major systems that can fail or wear. With the Bambino, you are primarily evaluating the espresso machine itself.

Why it makes sense used

The Bambino is particularly attractive if you already own—or intend to buy—a dedicated espresso grinder.

Separating your grinder and espresso machine also makes future upgrades easier. If the grinder becomes the limiting factor, replace the grinder. If you eventually want a different espresso machine, your grinder remains useful.

What to inspect

Ask the seller:

  • Does water flow normally through the group head?
  • Does the steam wand produce strong, consistent steam?
  • Has the machine been descaled according to its manual and water conditions?
  • Does it leak from underneath while brewing?
  • Are the portafilter and filter baskets included?
  • Are there any error lights or unusual noises?

Missing accessories are not necessarily a deal-breaker, but include their replacement cost when comparing listings.

2. Breville Bambino Plus BES500: best used option for frequent lattes and cappuccinos

The Bambino Plus keeps much of the Bambino's basic espresso architecture but adds a significant convenience feature: hands-free automatic milk texturing.

Breville's current Bambino Plus uses a 54mm portafilter, ThermoJet heating, PID temperature control, and selectable milk temperature and texture settings. The automatic steaming system is the real reason to choose the Plus over the standard Bambino. See Breville's Bambino Plus specifications.

That also creates an additional component to investigate when buying used.

Why it makes sense used

A discounted Bambino Plus can be particularly appealing for someone who regularly makes:

  • lattes,
  • cappuccinos,
  • flat whites,
  • or other milk-based drinks.

If you mostly drink straight espresso or americanos, the standard Bambino may offer better value.

What to inspect

The seller should be able to demonstrate that:

  • the steam wand works,
  • automatic steaming activates correctly,
  • the milk-temperature sensing system functions,
  • the wand is clean rather than blocked with dried milk,
  • and the machine completes normal brewing cycles.

A machine advertised simply as “powers on” is not enough.

You want evidence that it brews and steams.

3. Breville Barista Express BES870: best used all-in-one value

The Barista Express is a different buying decision.

Rather than pairing a compact espresso machine with a separate grinder, the Barista Express incorporates a conical burr grinder directly into the machine.

Breville's current BES870 uses a 54mm portafilter, integrated grinder, PID-controlled Thermocoil heating system, manual steam wand, volumetric shot control, and low-pressure pre-infusion followed by 9-bar extraction. See Breville's Barista Express specifications. For an older used unit, confirm its exact model and revision; current specifications should not be treated as proof of an older machine's configuration.

For someone starting from zero, that all-in-one design is convenient.

For someone buying used, however, it means you need to evaluate both the grinder and espresso machine.

What to check before buying

Do not buy based only on photographs of a clean stainless-steel exterior.

Ask the seller whether:

  1. The grinder runs without abnormal noises.
  2. The grind adjustment works throughout its range.
  3. Beans feed normally through the hopper.
  4. The machine reaches pressure and pulls shots normally.
  5. The steam wand works correctly.
  6. The hot-water function works.
  7. There are leaks around the group head or underneath the machine.
  8. The removable burr and hopper are included.

Also inspect the listing carefully for language such as:

  • “untested,”
  • “powers on,”
  • “for parts,”
  • “needs cleaning,”
  • or “I don't know anything about espresso machines.”

Those listings may still be worthwhile for someone comfortable repairing equipment, but they should not be valued like confirmed working machines.

Used vs. refurbished vs. open box: which should you choose?

These terms should not be treated as interchangeable.

Open box

Potentially the lowest-risk discount if the machine is effectively unused or lightly handled.

Still confirm exactly what is included. An open-box espresso machine missing a portafilter or other proprietary accessory can become less attractive quickly.

Refurbished

“Refurbished” can mean different things depending on who performed the work.

Read the listing rather than relying on the label.

Look for:

  • who refurbished it,
  • what was tested,
  • the return period,
  • warranty information,
  • and exactly what accessories are included.

Pre-owned or used

Used can provide the largest savings, but condition varies dramatically.

A well-maintained machine owned by someone who regularly cleaned and descaled it could be a better purchase than a cosmetically perfect machine that sat full of hard water.

The seven things I would check before buying any used espresso machine on eBay

1. Does it actually brew?

“Powers on” does not mean “works.”

Ask whether the seller has pulled a shot or at minimum run water through the group head.

2. Does it steam?

Steam problems matter especially on machines bought primarily for lattes and cappuccinos.

3. Is it leaking?

Look for seller comments or photographs showing water underneath the machine, around fittings, or around the group head.

4. Has it been descaled?

Scale is one of the major unknowns in a used coffee machine because exterior condition tells you almost nothing about the condition of the water path.

5. What accessories are missing?

Check for the:

  • portafilter,
  • single and double baskets,
  • milk jug,
  • water tank,
  • bean hopper on grinder-equipped machines,
  • burr components,
  • cleaning components,
  • and tamper.

You can replace many of these, but replacements change the true purchase price.

6. What is the total delivered price?

Do not compare listing prices alone.

A machine priced $40 cheaper but carrying $60 more shipping is not the cheaper machine.

Compare:

item price + shipping + applicable tax + immediately necessary replacement parts

7. Can you return it?

The more complicated and expensive the machine, the more valuable a reasonable return policy becomes.

A return window gives you an opportunity to verify brewing, steaming, grinder operation, leaks, controls, and other functions after delivery. Check the deadline, return-shipping responsibility, and any conditions before buying.

What I would avoid

I would be cautious about listings with any of these characteristics:

  • No evidence the machine has been tested
  • “Powers on” as the only condition information
  • Heavy visible scale or residue
  • Missing water tank
  • Missing grinder components on Barista models
  • Major rust or corrosion
  • Seller cannot answer whether it brews
  • Extremely high shipping costs
  • Final price too close to a new machine
  • Parts-only machines unless you specifically want a repair project

The purpose of buying used is not simply to pay less upfront. It is to get more machine for the total amount of money you're spending.

One final rule: compare the machine, not just the discount

It is easy to see a large percentage discount and assume you found a deal.

That is backwards.

First decide which machine fits your routine. Then compare listings for that machine.

A cheap Barista Express is not a bargain if you actually wanted the simplicity and compact footprint of a Bambino. And a heavily discounted Bambino Plus is not useful if you already own a capable steam machine and never make milk drinks.

Start with the workflow. Then shop the used market.

FAQ

Is it safe to buy a used Breville espresso machine on eBay?

It can be, provided the seller clearly documents the machine's condition and you check brewing, steaming, maintenance history, included accessories, seller feedback, shipping costs, and return terms.

Which Breville espresso machine is best to buy used?

The Bambino is particularly appealing because it provides Breville's core espresso system without adding an integrated grinder that also needs to be evaluated. Buyers who want automatic milk texturing should consider the Bambino Plus.

Is a used Barista Express worth buying?

It can be if the savings are meaningful and both the grinder and espresso systems are confirmed working. Because it combines two major systems, it deserves more inspection than a Bambino.

Should I buy an eBay refurbished espresso machine instead of used?

A refurbished machine may reduce risk if the seller clearly explains who performed the refurbishment, what was tested, what warranty applies, and whether returns are accepted. Do not rely on the word “refurbished” by itself.

What accessories should come with a used Breville?

At minimum, determine whether the portafilter, filter baskets, water tank and machine-specific components are included. Grinder-equipped models should also have their hopper and necessary burr components. Missing items are not always a reason to skip a machine, but their replacement costs should be included in your comparison.
